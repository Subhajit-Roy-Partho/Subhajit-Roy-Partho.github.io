The goal here was to measure the distance between two ligands bound to a DNA phosphate backbone with as much accuracy as possible. Since the ligands included non-standard elements, classical all-atom MD wasn't a good fit, which pushed me toward quantum force fields instead.

1. Draw the ligand and DNA structures in Avogadro, which also does a rough initial optimization.
2. Pre-optimize the resulting `.cml` files with a classical force field (`MMFF94s`) before handing them to a quantum engine (GAMESS, Gaussian, etc.) — this saves the expensive quantum optimizer from doing all the work from a poor starting geometry. Using Open Babel:

```bash
obabel initial.cml -O optimized.cml --minimize --steps 15000 --sd --log --ff MMFF94s
```

- `initial.cml` / `optimized.cml` — input and output structures.
- `--sd` — use the steepest-descent algorithm.
- `--log` — log the whole minimization.

Available force fields:

```text
GAFF        General Amber Force Field (GAFF).
Ghemical    Ghemical force field.
MMFF94      MMFF94 force field.
MMFF94s     MMFF94s force field.
UFF         Universal Force Field.
```

## Choosing a basis set

Hand the pre-optimized geometry to the quantum engine with a Karlsruhe/Ahlrichs basis: `def2-SVP` (polarized valence double-ζ) for quick runs, `def2-TZVP` (triple-ζ) when you need more accuracy. The family is all-electron across H–Kr; for Rb–Rn use the matching `def2-ECP` effective core potentials. Definitions come from the Basis Set Exchange.

## Convergence criteria

For reference, ORCA's stock geometry-optimization presets:

```text
NormalOpt   TolE 5e-6 Eh · TolMaxG 3e-4 · TolRMSG 1e-4 · TolMaxD 4e-3 bohr · TolRMSD 2e-3
TightOpt    TolE 1e-6 Eh · TolMaxG 1e-4 · TolRMSG 3e-5 · TolMaxD 1e-3 bohr · TolRMSD 6e-4
```

## Open Babel & Avogadro

Open Babel 3 renamed the `babel` binary to `obabel`. The maintained minimization entry points are `obminimize` (`-ff <ffid>`, `-n <steps>`, `-c <crit>`) and the one-shot `obabel in -O out --minimize --ff <ffid>` used above. In Avogadro, the built-in minimizer lives under **Extensions → Optimize Geometry** (`Ctrl+Alt+O`), with the force field and convergence criteria configurable there. Note that MMFF94 and GAFF are not parameterized for charged or radical species.
